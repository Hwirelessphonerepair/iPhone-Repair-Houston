iPhone Not Charging? How to Find the Real Cause Before You Pay for a Repair

By H Wireless Phone Repair, Houston, TX · October 1, 2026 · 8-minute read

You plug in your iPhone before bed, and in the morning it shows the same percentage it had the night before. Or the lightning-bolt icon flickers on and off whenever the cable moves. When an iPhone stops charging, most people assume the port or the battery has failed. Often, though, the problem is a worn cable, a weak adapter, lint in the port or a software setting.

The cable, adapter, port, battery and software all play a part in charging, and liquid in the connector can stop it on purpose. Check them in the order below and you can fix many problems in minutes. You'll also know what to tell a technician if the fault turns out to be hardware. If the phone still won't charge after these checks, the next step is a hands-on diagnosis. A shop that offers iPhone repair in Houston can test the cable, port and battery separately. That way you replace only the part that is actually at fault.

Start With the Free Checks: Cable, Adapter and Outlet

Begin with the cheapest parts of the setup. Charging cables bend thousands of times, and the wires inside usually break near the connector ends, where you can't see the damage. A cable that still looks fine can carry too little power to charge the phone, or lose the connection whenever it moves.

  1. Try a different cable and adapter. Use a cable and adapter you know work with another phone. If your iPhone charges right away, you've found the problem.
  2. Try a different power source. Plug into a wall outlet you know works, not a laptop port, car charger or power strip. Some USB ports don't supply enough power to charge a phone in use.
  3. Read any alert on the screen. Apple says an "accessory not supported or certified" message can mean the charging accessory is defective or damaged, or that the USB charger isn't designed to charge devices.
  4. Test wireless charging if your model supports it. If the phone charges on a Qi-certified pad but not with a cable, the battery is probably accepting power. That points to the cable, adapter or port.

Restart the Phone and Give It Time

Sometimes the hardware is fine and the software has frozen. Apple's troubleshooting steps for an iPhone that won't charge start with a restart. Plug the phone in and let it charge for half an hour. If it still won't respond or only shows a battery icon, force restart it, then let it charge for another 30 minutes.

On iPhone 8 and later models, a force restart works like this: press and release volume up, press and release volume down, then press and hold the side button until the Apple logo appears. A fully drained phone can stay dark for several minutes after you plug it in.

Is It Really Broken? When Charging "Stops" at 80%

Many people report a charging fault when the phone is actually working as designed. Two features can hold an iPhone at about 80%:

  • Battery protection. Apple notes that an iPhone may get slightly warm while charging. If the battery gets too warm, software may limit charging above 80% until the temperature drops. Moving the phone and charger somewhere cooler usually fixes this.
  • Charge limits and optimized charging. iPhone 15 models and later can be set to stop at a chosen limit, and Optimized Battery Charging can hold the battery at 80% overnight. You can check both in the Battery section of Settings.

Look Inside the Charging Port

Pocket lint is one of the most common reasons an iPhone stops charging. Each time you plug in, the cable packs debris further into the port until the plug can't seat fully. The usual signs are a cable that feels loose, no longer clicks in, or charges only when held at an angle.

Turn the phone off and shine a flashlight into the port. If you see a gray or dark layer at the back, debris is a likely cause. Don't scrape it out with a metal pin or needle. The contacts inside are small and easy to bend or short. A technician can clear the port with the right tools and check whether the contacts are worn, corroded or loose. "Liquid Detected": What to Do and What Not to Do

iPhone XS, XS Max, XR and later models can detect liquid in the Lightning or USB-C connector. When they do, cable charging is disabled until the port, cable and accessory are dry. That can look like a charging fault, but the phone is protecting itself. According to Apple, charging while the connector is wet can corrode the pins on the port or cable and cause permanent damage.

Apple's recommended steps are straightforward:

  1. Unplug the cable from both the phone and the adapter.
  2. Gently tap the iPhone against your hand with the connector facing down to remove excess liquid.
  3. Leave the phone in a dry place with some airflow, and try charging again after at least 30 minutes.
  4. If the alert comes back, keep waiting. Apple says the phone may take up to 24 hours to dry fully.
Avoid these common mistakes. Apple advises against drying an iPhone with an external heat source or compressed air. Don't push a cotton swab or paper towel into the connector. Apple also says not to put the phone in a bag of rice, because small rice particles can damage it. If the back of the phone is dry, you can still charge it on a Qi-certified wireless charger.

If the alert appears with only one cable, the cable may be damaged. If it appears every time with genuine Apple cables, even after the phone has been dry for a full day, there may be moisture or corrosion inside the phone. A technician should inspect it.

When the Battery Is the Real Problem

Sometimes the phone charges, but the battery can't hold the charge. Lithium-ion batteries lose capacity with every charge cycle. Apple rates iPhone 14 and earlier models to keep about 80% of their original capacity after 500 full charge cycles under ideal conditions. It rates iPhone 15 and later models for 1,000 cycles. Heat, heavy use and age can all lower capacity sooner.

You can check the battery's Maximum Capacity in the Battery Health section of Settings. On newer models, the cycle count is shown there too. A worn battery often causes the following symptoms:

  • The phone goes from a high percentage to empty much faster than it used to.
  • It shuts down unexpectedly, sometimes with 20% or 30% still showing.
  • It only stays on while plugged in.
  • The percentage jumps around or seems stuck while charging.

If you see these symptoms and the Maximum Capacity is low, a battery replacement is usually a better choice than replacing the whole phone. A battery that is swelling is different. It is a safety issue, not an inconvenience.

Stop using the phone if you notice any of these: the screen lifts away from the frame, the back bulges, the phone gets unusually hot while charging, or it smells like burning. Unplug it, don't try to charge it again, and have it inspected. A damaged lithium-ion battery shouldn't be pressed, punctured or left charging.

When to Bring It to a Technician

Get professional help if you've tried a known-good cable and adapter, restarted the phone, ruled out liquid and charge limits, and it still won't charge reliably. The same applies if you see corrosion, a loose port, swelling or a phone that won't turn on.

Bring your own cable and adapter. If the phone charges at the shop but not at home, that tells the technician where to look. Back up the phone if it still turns on, and mention any recent drop, spill or new charger. A good shop should explain what it found before starting work. A charging complaint might need a port cleaning, a new battery or a port replacement, and each costs a different amount.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my iPhone charge only when I hold the cable at an angle?

The usual causes are lint packed into the port, a cable with broken wires near the connector, or worn port contacts. Try a different cable first. If that doesn't help, have the port inspected and cleaned before replacing anything.

Is it safe to use cheap third-party chargers?

Apple notes that some USB chargers aren't designed to charge devices and that defective accessories can trigger an "accessory not supported" alert. Choose a reputable, certified cable and adapter. If a charger gets unusually hot or smells, stop using it.

Does a charging problem always mean I need a new charging port?

No. Many charging problems turn out to be a bad cable, debris in the port, a setting or a worn battery. A proper diagnosis checks each of these before recommending a port replacement.

The Bottom Line

Most iPhone charging problems come from a small set of causes, and many of them cost nothing to fix. Check them in order: cable and adapter, restart, settings and temperature, the port, liquid, then the battery. Once you've ruled out the simple causes, an in-person diagnosis can find the actual fault. That way you pay to repair only the part that has failed.
